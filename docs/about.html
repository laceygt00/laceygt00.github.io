About

Hi, I’m Lacey —
nice to meet you.

I’m a graduate student who discovered biostatistics after deciding I did not want to pursue the medical field.

The Path Here
Before
Started in Biology on the Pre-Medicine Track

In my undergraduate I graduated with a B.S. in Biology with an emphasis in Cell Biology and Physiology. I completed all the Pre-medical class work with the intention of becoming a doctor.

Turning point
Decided I did not want to be a doctor

I was working as a medical assistant in a dermatology office during my gap year when I realized that I did not want to pursue this path. I had always wanted to move out to Seattle, so I found a job and packed up for the first time in my life.

After
Discovered Biostatistics

I was working as an office manager at a criminal defense firm while trying to fgiure out what I wanted to do. I met someone who worked in computational biology which led me to biostatistics. I realized biostatistics merged everything I valued: the logic and beauty of mathematics, the scientific depth of biology, and the opportunity to make a real-world impact on health and society.

Now
Biostatistics at the University of Washington

I took the pre-requisite classes for my program and was fortunate to get into my dream program. I am learning a lot, exploring this new field while finding my passions, and loving the process of it all.

A Few Things About Me
I learn by doing

Whether it’s a new modeling framework or a new programming language, I always find it easier to just build something and work backwards from the errors.

I care about communication

A result you can’t explain is only half a result. Building dashboards and visualizations is my way of making sure the work actually reaches the people who need it.

I’m currently looking

I’m actively exploring positions in data science, biostatistics, and research analytics — anywhere I can keep doing work that matters and keep learning.

Let’s Work Together

I’m always happy to chat about research, data science, career opportunities, or interesting problems you’re working on.
